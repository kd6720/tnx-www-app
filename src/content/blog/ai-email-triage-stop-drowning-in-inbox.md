The average knowledge worker spends 28% of their workweek on email. That's over eleven hours. For people in client-facing roles — sales, support, account management — it's often more. And most of those hours aren't spent on the handful of emails that actually move the needle. They're spent sorting, categorizing, deleting, and writing the same "let me check on that for you" reply for the fourth time today.

AI email triage changes that equation.

## What AI Email Triage Actually Does

At its core, AI email triage does three things:

**Classify.** The AI reads every incoming email and tags it: urgent, client-facing, internal, spam, newsletter, invoice, support request. It knows the difference between "the contract is ready to sign" and "here's this week's newsletter."

**Route.** Once classified, emails go where they belong. Client requests hit the right account manager. Support tickets land in the ticketing system. Invoices go to AP. Newsletters get archived or summarized.

**Draft.** For common response patterns, the AI drafts a reply. You review, tweak if needed, and send. It's not about replacing your voice — it's about not typing the same thing 20 times a day.

## The Tools That Make It Work

You don't need a custom-built solution. The ecosystem is mature:

**Microsoft Copilot for Outlook** — Built into Microsoft 365. It summarizes threads, drafts replies in your tone, and can be configured with rules for routing. If your organization already uses M365, this is the path of least resistance.

**Google Gemini in Gmail** — Similar capabilities for Google Workspace users. Side-panel summarization, smart reply suggestions, and the ability to pull context from your Drive and Calendar.

**Superhuman** — An email client built on top of Gmail and Outlook that uses AI for triage, scheduled sending, and follow-up reminders. It's fast, keyboard-driven, and splits your inbox into streams automatically.

**Front** — Shared inbox platform with AI-powered routing rules, sentiment analysis, and canned response suggestions. Built for teams handling high volume.

**Custom via API** — For organizations with niche needs, OpenAI's API or Anthropic's Claude can be connected to email via Zapier or Make to create custom triage pipelines. Incoming email → classify → route → draft reply. All automated.

## What to Automate First

Don't try to automate everything at once. Start with the emails you genuinely don't need to see:

- **Newsletters and promotions.** AI can identify these with near-perfect accuracy. Auto-archive them, or better yet, have the AI generate a one-paragraph daily digest instead of 20 individual emails.

- **Meeting scheduling.** "What times work for you next week?" — the AI can check your calendar and propose three slots before you even see the email.

- **Status requests.** "Where are we on the Johnson proposal?" If your CRM or project management tool is integrated, the AI can pull the actual status and draft a response.

- **Password resets and access requests.** These are pure process. Route them to IT, draft the standard reply, move on.

## Where the Human Stays Essential

AI email triage works best when you're clear about what it *shouldn't* handle:

- **Sensitive client escalations.** If a client is frustrated, they need a human. AI can flag it and surface it immediately — but the response should come from a person.

- **Negotiation threads.** Pricing discussions, contract terms, scope changes — these require judgment AI doesn't have yet.

- **Relationship-building emails.** The "just checking in, how was the conference" email is better written by you. AI can remind you to send it, but don't outsource the warmth.

## The ROI

Let's do the math. If you spend 11 hours a week on email and AI triage cuts that by 40%, you reclaim 4.4 hours per week. That's 228 hours a year — nearly six full workweeks. For a team of ten, it's the equivalent of hiring an additional person, without the salary.

The tools above range from free (built into M365/Google Workspace) to roughly $30/user/month. Even at the high end, you're buying back time at under $2/hour.

## Getting Started This Week

1. **Audit your inbox for two days.** Tag every email as "needed my attention" or "didn't need my attention." You'll probably find 30-50% fall into the second bucket.

2. **Pick one category to automate.** Newsletters, scheduling, or status requests — whichever one annoys you most.

3. **Turn it on.** If you're on M365, Copilot is already there. If you're on Gmail, enable Gemini. Start with the built-in tools before shopping for add-ons.

4. **Review for a week.** Check what the AI classified and routed. Adjust rules. Refine prompts. It gets better with feedback.

Email doesn't have to be the thing that eats your mornings. AI triage won't make your inbox empty — but it'll make sure the only emails that reach you are the ones that actually need you.
